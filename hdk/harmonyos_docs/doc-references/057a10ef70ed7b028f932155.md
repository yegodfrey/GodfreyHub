TransferQueue
    
    public interface TransferQueue<E>
    extends [BlockingQueue](https://developer.harmonyos.com/cn/docs/documentation/doc-references/blockingqueue-0000001054838560)<E>  
  
---  
A [BlockingQueue](https://developer.harmonyos.com/cn/docs/documentation/doc-references/blockingqueue-0000001054838560) in which producers may wait for consumers to receive elements. A TransferQueue may be useful for example in message passing applications in which producers sometimes (using method [transfer(E)](https://developer.harmonyos.com/cn/docs/documentation/doc-references/transferqueue-0000001054678610#ZH-CN_TOPIC_0000001054678610__transfer-E-)) await receipt of elements by consumers invoking take or poll, while at other times enqueue elements (via method put) without waiting for receipt. [Non-blocking](https://developer.harmonyos.com/cn/docs/documentation/doc-references/transferqueue-0000001054678610#ZH-CN_TOPIC_0000001054678610__tryTransfer-E-) and [time-out](https://developer.harmonyos.com/cn/docs/documentation/doc-references/transferqueue-0000001054678610#ZH-CN_TOPIC_0000001054678610__tryTransfer-E-long-java_util_concurrent_TimeUnit-) versions of tryTransfer are also available. A TransferQueue may also be queried, via [hasWaitingConsumer()](https://developer.harmonyos.com/cn/docs/documentation/doc-references/transferqueue-0000001054678610#ZH-CN_TOPIC_0000001054678610__hasWaitingConsumer--), whether there are any threads waiting for items, which is a converse analogy to a peek operation. Like other blocking queues, a TransferQueue may be capacity bounded. If so, an attempted transfer operation may initially block waiting for available space, and/or subsequently block waiting for reception by a consumer. Note that in a queue with zero capacity, such as [SynchronousQueue](https://developer.harmonyos.com/cn/docs/documentation/doc-references/synchronousqueue-0000001054439901), put and transfer are effectively synonymous.
