# Handling Audio Data Buffer

#### Scenario

During a wireless connection, background scanning and spatial interference may cause delayed or repeated data transmission. As a result, frame freezing may occur during audio playback.

To avoid audio intermittence, you need to delay the playback of non-real-time audio data streams (such as music) during a wireless connection. For data with high real-time requirements, such as calls, voice advertising, and beep prompting sound, no processing is required.

You are advised to set the buffer delay to 500 ms (25 frames) and the maximum buffer delay to 600 ms. (The maximum buffer delay is used to suppress greater jitter but does not increase the delay.) If the buffer delay exceeds the maximum buffer delay, packet loss will occur.

#### Development Procedure

     1. Initialize the playback delay time (corresponding to a buffer). It is recommended that the playback delay time be 500 ms (25 frames).
     2. When receiving a data stream, determine whether to delay the playback.

For data that does not require delay, refer to the following criteria:

        * Calls: **StreamType** = **0** , including carrier calls, MeeTime calls, and VoIP calls
        * Voice advertising: **usage** = **65536**
        * Beep prompting sound: **voiceState** = **1** and **usage** for subsequently received audio data = **0**
     3. For the data that needs to be delayed, wait until the playback delay time elapses and retrieve the data from the buffer for playback. If the jitter during subsequent playback is less than the specified delay, intermittence will not occur. (Note: Data is cached in the buffer during the entire process.)
