The HiCar SDK uses the Android audio focus mechanism to resolve the conflicts between HiCar and the native audio app of the head unit.

#### Audio Focus Solution

In the HiCar audio focus solution, the mobile phone and head unit are considered as a whole. When a mobile phone app applies for an audio focus on the mobile phone side, the HiCar synchronously applies for an audio focus on the head unit side through the proxy. Conversely, when applying for an audio focus on the head unit, it also needs to synchronously apply for an audio focus on the mobile phone by using the proxy.

#### Audio Stream Types

The HiCar SDK defines the following audio stream types: media, ringtone, voice call, alarm, notification tone, and others.

#### Audio Focus Types

After a HiCar connection is set up between the phone and the head unit, HiCar will request the audio focus before playing the audio. The head unit also needs to notify HiCar of the current audio focus status by using the audio focus notification.

For the audio focus types that can be applied by HiCar while playing audio on the head unit, please refer to Table 2.

The head unit sends notification of the audio focus change when a native app is playing videos. For the corresponding focus change notifications, please refer to Table 3.

**Table 2** Audio focus types for HiCar audio playbackEntity | Focus Type | Method | Description  
---|---|---|---  
Mobile Phone | Permanent focus | AUDIOFOCUS_GAIN | HiCar requests to occupy audio focus for an extended period of time. If focus is lost, the playback stops and the focus is released.  
Transient focus | AUDIOFOCUS_GAIN_TRANSIENT | HiCar requests to occupy audio focus for a short period of time. If focus is lost, the playback is paused.  
Duck focus | AUDIOFOCUS_GAIN_TRANSIENT_MAY_DUCK | HiCar requests to occupy audio focus for a short period of time. If focus is lost, the playback can go on but the volume should be turned down or the playback should be paused.  
Exclusive and transient audio focus | AUDIOFOCUS_GAIN_TRANSIENT_EXCLUSIVE | HiCar requests to occupy audio focus for a short period of time. Other audio sources that don't have focus are expected to be muted.  
Releasing transient focus or transient focus with ducking | AUDIOFOCUS_RELEASE | HiCar requests for the release of audio focus.  
  
When HiCar applies for the audio focus, the manufacturer needs to perform the following operations:

     * When HiCar applies for transient focus with transient Duck focus, perform sound reduction and mixing for the audio content of all local multimedia apps.
     * When HiCar applies for transient focus, pause the audio of all local multimedia apps. After HiCar releases the focus, resume the playback.
     * When HiCar applies for permanent focus with ducking, pause the audio of all local multimedia apps.

**Table 3** Focus change notifications sent by the head unit to HiCarEntity | Focus Type | Focus Change | Description  
---|---|---|---  
Head unit | Permanent focus | AUDIOFOCUS_LOSS | HiCar loses audio focus or cannot obtain audio focus.  
Transient focus | AUDIOFOCUS_LOSS_TRANSIENT | HiCar temporarily loses audio focus.  
Duck focus | AUDIOFOCUS_LOSS_TRANSIENT_CAN_DUCK | If the volume is decreased, HiCar can continue to play through the media channel.  
Releasing transient focus or transient focus with ducking | AUDIOFOCUS_GAIN | HiCar obtains audio focus.  
  
HiCar obtains the focus change event from the head unit through the proxy and transfers the event to the mobile phone to control the app on the mobile phone. Therefore, when the local apps on the head unit apply for the audio focus, pay attention to the following:

     * For services that require HiCar audio stop, such as local music and FM, apply for permanent focus on the head unit side.
     * For services that require HiCar audio suspension and resumption, such as local short video, voice interaction, and call, apply for transient focus on the head unit.
     * For services that require audio mixing by decreasing the HiCar audio volume, such as local navigation broadcast and notification sound, apply for Duck focus on the head unit.

#### Enabling Audio Stream Distribution

In distributed service scenarios, audio data is mixed on the phone, and then transmitted from the phone to the head unit through the Wi-Fi channel for playback on the head unit. After audio stream distribution is enabled, navigation streams, media streams, and call streams are directly transmitted to the head unit without being mixed, and then written into different **AudioTrack**. Other types of audio streams are mixed by the phone before being transmitted to the head unit.

After the SDK is successfully initialized, the head unit can call the API to enable the audio stream distribution policy. Note that audio stream distribution requires mobile phones running HarmonyOS 3.0 or later.

**Table 4** APIs for enabling audio stream distributionClass | Method | Description  
---|---|---  
HiCarAdapter | enableMultiAudioBusPolicy | Enables audio stream distribution.  
  
#### Processing Customized Audio Policies (Beta)

If you need to customize the audio policies, refer to the following content in this section. If customization is not required, you can skip this section.

After the audio data is transmitted from the phone to the head unit, the HiCar SDK uses the **AudioTrack** to play the audio data. When the audio data is played on the head unit, the HiCar SDK maps the audio data according to the audio conversion policy defined by Huawei. Considering that different vendors have different mapping mechanisms for audio policies, the HiCar SDK allows the customization of audio policies. The HiCar SDK reports the original audio parameters (such as **Usage** , **ContentType** , and **focusGain**) of the audio data obtained from the phone to the HMI app, which will construct **AudioAttributes** or **StreamType** based on the original information mapping and return **AudioAttributes** or **StreamType** to the HiCar SDK. The HiCar SDK then applies for the focus based on the mapped **AudioAttributes** or **StreamType** to manage the focus or creates an **AudioTrack** to play audio streams.

The process involves the following two scenarios:

     * Focus application: There is a single **Usage** , and the HMI app directly maps and constructs **AudioAttribute** or **StreamType**.
     * Audio stream playback: In the single-audio-source scenario, there is a single **Usage** and **contentType** , and **AudioAttributes** or **StreamType** can be directly mapped and constructed. In the multi-audio-source mixing scenario, there are multiple valid **Usage** and **contentType** values In this case, determine the mixing scenario based on **Usage** , and construct an appropriate **AudioAttributes** or **StreamType**.

**Table 5** APIs for audio policy customizationClass | Method | Description  
---|---|---  
CarAudioListener | getCustomizedAudioAttributes | Returns **AudioAttributes** or **StreamType** that is constructed by the HMI app based on the original information to the HiCar SDK. When the HMI app receives the callback, pay attention to the **focusGain** parameter:
     * **0** : Callback for audio playback, indicating the **streamtype** or **AudioAttributes** for audio playback needs to be obtained. The HMI app needs to construct **streamtype** or **AudioAttributes** according to **usage** in the passed parameters. Based on the constructed **streamtype** or **AudioAttributes** , instantiate and return **CustomizedAudioAttributes**.
     * **-1** : Callback for audio focus release. This operation only notifies the HMI app that the focus is released. The HMI app does not need to pay attention to other parameters. After obtaining the callback for focus release, the HMI app only needs to perform the corresponding operation and return any value.
     * Other values (**1** , **2** , **3** , and **4**): Callbacks for audio focus application, indicating the **streamtype** or **AudioAttributes** for focus application needs to be obtained. The HMI app needs to construct **streamtype** or **AudioAttributes** according to **usage** in the passed parameters, or according to both **usage** and the value of **focusGain**. Based on the constructed **streamtype** or **AudioAttributes** , instantiate and return **CustomizedAudioAttributes**.  
HiCarAdapter | registerCarAudioListener | The HMI app should register the listener for audio events when initializing the SDK.  
unRegisterCarAudioListener | The HMI app should deregister the listener for audio events when deinitializing the SDK.  
  
To customize an audio policy, the HMI app must register the audio event listener during SDK initialization and implement the customized audio policy in the callback.

     1. The HMI app should implement the customized **CarAudioListener**.

The following is the sample code:
            
            public class HiCarAudioListener extends CarAudioListener {
            	@Override
            	public CustomizedAudioAttributes getCustomizedAudioAttributes(int[] usage, int[] contentType,
            		int focusGain, Map<String, String> info) {
            		Log.i(TAG, "getCustomizedAudioAttributes.");
            		CustomizedAudioAttributes customizedAudioAttributes;
            		// If there are multiple **usages** , determine the value of **usage** before constructing **streamType**.
            		int usage = usages[0];
            		int streamType = usageToStreamType(usage);
                            // When the voice is reported, the value of **usage** may be **17** , referring to a Huawei-specific voice type. You need to convert the voice type to another voice type in Android by changing the value of **usage**. In the sample code, **usage** is converted to **USAGE_ASSISTANT**.
                            if (usage == 17) {
                                   usage = AudioAttributes.USAGE_ASSISTANT;
                            }
            		int contentType = streamType2ContentType(streamType);
            		if (Build.VERSION.SDK_INT > Build.VERSION_CODES.O) {
            			AudioAttributes audioAttributes = new AudioAttributes.Builder().setUsage(usage)
            					.setContentType(contentType).build();
            			customizedAudioAttributes = new CustomizedAudioAttributes(audioAttributes,
            					focusGain, map);
            		} else {
            			customizedAudioAttributes = new CustomizedAudioAttributes(streamType, focusGain, map);
            		}
            		Log.i(TAG, "getCustomizedAudioAttributes, mCustomizedAudioAttributes:"
            				+ customizedAudioAttributes.toString());
            		return customizedAudioAttributes;
            	}
            }

If audio policy customization is not required, you do not need to call **registerCarAudioListener** to register the audio event listener. Alternatively, directly return a null value in the callback of **getCustomizedAudioAttributes**. The HiCar SDK then uses the audio policy customized by HiCar for mapping.

     2. Register the audio event listener during the initialization of the HiCar SDK.

The following is the sample code:
            
            int carAudioRerCode = mHiCarAdapter.registerCarAudioListener(mHiCarAudioListener);
            if (carAudioRerCode != HiCarConst.SUCCESS) {
            	Log.d(TAG, "HiCar reg audio listener failed");
            }

     3. Cancel the audio event listener during the deinitialization of the HiCar SDK.

The following is the sample code:
            
            int carListenerRetCode = mHiCarAdapter.unRegisterCarListener(mHiCarListener);
            if (carListenerRetCode != HiCarConst.SUCCESS) {
            	Log.d(TAG, "HiCar unreg listener failed");
            }
